En or et? How to know the gender of Danish nouns

A1–A2 5 min read · Updated 6 October 2026

Short answer

Every Danish noun is either an en-word (en bil, a car) or an et-word (et hus, a house). Roughly three in four are en-words. A few endings give the gender away, compound words follow their last part, and for the rest the best habit is to learn each noun together with its article.

Why it matters

Getting en or et wrong rarely stops people understanding you, but the gender travels through the whole phrase. It decides three things:

en-wordet-word
Articleen bilet hus
Definite formbilenhuset
Adjectiveen stor bilet stort hus
Pronounden er røddet er rødt

Endings that give the gender away

These are the most useful patterns. They do not cover every noun, but when a word has one of these endings, you can be fairly confident.

EndingGenderExamples
-hedenen mulighed, en sandhed, en frihed
-ing / -ningenen regning, en parkering, en kylling
-tion / -sionenen station, en situation, en diskussion
-domenen sygdom, en ejendom
-umetet museum, et centrum, et gymnasium
-mentetet dokument, et instrument, et parlament
-erietet bageri, et bryggeri
-skabmostly etet venskab, et selskab, et landskab (but en egenskab, en videnskab)

People and animals are usually en

Nouns for people and animals are mostly en-words: en mand, en kvinde, en lærer, en hund, en kat. A handful of very common exceptions are worth learning by heart: et barn (a child), et menneske (a human being), et dyr (an animal).

Compound words follow their last part

Danish builds long words by joining shorter ones. The gender always comes from the last part:

  • et hus → et sommerhusa house → a summer house
  • en skole → en sprogskolea school → a language school
  • et skab → et køleskaba cupboard → a fridge

Quick check

___ sommerhus (a summer house)

et sommerhus. The last part is et hus, so the whole word is an et-word.

Show answer

et sommerhus. The last part, hus, is an et-word.

The short et-words you just have to learn

Many everyday et-words are short and follow no pattern. These come up constantly, so they are worth memorising first: et hus, et bord, et æble, et år, et sted, et land, et ord, et billede, et vindue.

How to learn it for good

  • Never learn a bare noun. Learn et æble, not æble.
  • Learn it with the definite form: en bil – bilen, et hus – huset. Hearing the ending helps it stick.
  • When in doubt, say en. With roughly three in four nouns being en-words, it is the safer guess.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a rule for en and et in Danish?

There is no single rule, but some endings are reliable: -hed, -ing and -tion words are en-words, and -um and -ment words are et-words. For everything else, learn each noun together with its article.

Are most Danish nouns en or et?

Most are en-words: roughly three in four. If you have to guess, guess en.

Why does en or et matter?

The gender changes the ending of the definite form (bilen, huset), the adjective (en stor bil, et stort hus) and the pronoun you use for the noun (den or det).

What about compound words?

A compound word takes the gender of its last part: et hus gives et sommerhus, and en skole gives en sprogskole.
