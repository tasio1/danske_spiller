Where does ikke go? Danish word order in subordinate clauses

B1–B2 4 min read · Updated 6 October 2026

Short answer

In a normal Danish sentence, ikke comes after the verb: Jeg spiser ikke kød. In a subordinate clause, one that starts with a word like fordi, at, hvis or om, it jumps in front of the verb: …, fordi jeg ikke spiser kød.

Main clause: verb first, then ikke

In a sentence that can stand on its own, the verb comes first and ikke follows it. That stays true when the subject is pushed behind the verb by inversion.

  • Jegsubject
  • spiserverb
  • ikkethen ikke
  • kødthe rest
  • Jeg spiser ikke kød.I don't eat meat.
  • I dag spiser jeg ikke kød.Today I'm not eating meat. (verb, subject, then ikke)

Subordinate clause: ikke jumps in front

A subordinate clause is a half-sentence that hangs on another one. In Danish it starts with a signal word, and inside it the order changes: subject, ikke, verb.

  • fordisignal
  • jegsubject
  • ikkeikke first
  • spiserverb
  • kødthe rest
  • Hun laver salat, fordi jeg ikke spiser kød.She's making a salad because I don't eat meat.
  • Det er ærgerligt, at du ikke kan komme.It's a shame you can't come.
  • Jeg bliver glad, hvis det ikke regner.I'll be happy if it doesn't rain.
  • Hun laver salat, fordi jeg spiser ikke kød.Main-clause order inside a fordi-clause.

With two verbs, ikke goes in front of the first one: …, fordi jeg ikke har set filmen.

Quick check

“I'm staying home because I don't have time.”

… fordi jeg ikke har tid. After fordi the order is subject, ikke, verb.

Show answer

… fordi jeg ikke har tid. After fordi: subject, ikke, verb.

How to spot a subordinate clause

Look for the signal word at the start. The common ones:

  • at (that), fordi (because), hvis (if), når (when), selvom (even though), mens (while), før (before)
  • som and der (who, which): en film, som jeg aldrig har set
  • om (whether) and question words in indirect questions: Jeg ved ikke, hvorfor han ikke ringede.

It's not only ikke

The same small words follow the same rule: altid (always), aldrig (never), også (also), kun (only), gerne (gladly), ofte (often) and allerede (already). After the verb in a main clause, before the verb in a subordinate clause.

  • Peter drikker aldrig kaffe.Main clause: verb, then aldrig.
  • Jeg ved, at Peter aldrig drikker kaffe.After at: aldrig, then the verb.
  • Hun siger, at hun altid kommer til tiden.She says she's always on time.

Quick check

“She's asking whether you're coming too.”

… om du også kommer. om starts a subordinate clause, so også goes in front of the verb, just like ikke.

Show answer

Hun spørger, om du også kommer. After om, også goes before the verb.

Quick check

A normal main clause: “Peter never drinks coffee.”

Peter drikker aldrig kaffe. No signal word, so this is a main clause: verb first, then aldrig.

Show answer

Peter drikker aldrig kaffe. Main clause: verb first, then aldrig.

What you may hear in speech

After verbs of saying and thinking, Danes sometimes use main-clause order in an at-clause when they speak, for example Han sagde, at han kunne ikke komme. You will hear it, but in writing and in exams, stick to the rule: …, at han ikke kunne komme.
